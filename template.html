01 / Research

Auditing autonomous agents
& foundations of superintelligence.

I connect the evaluation of today's AI systems with research on AGI and ASI risks. Preparing for more capable intelligence means testing its limits, understanding failure modes, and keeping people involved in consequential decisions.

Featured research / AGI & ASI

AGI concerns broadly capable intelligence across tasks; ASI concerns intelligence exceeding human cognitive capabilities. My focus is preparation: evaluating capabilities and risks, strengthening safeguards, and educating people to question and verify what increasingly capable systems do.

Doctoral research / Rutgers University

As an NSF CSGrad4US Fellow, I work with Dov Kruger in Electrical and Computer Engineering, specializing in machine learning. My 2025–2026 work spans compression for AI training, distributed data-poisoning benchmarks, multimodal applications, and accessible STEM learning.

Explore the research domains

ASI risk & containment architecture

My research agenda connects AGI/ASI risk analysis with runtime monitoring, tool-authorization boundaries, and adversarial testing of prompt-injection defenses. The goal is to make the limits of autonomous systems measurable and their consequential actions reviewable.

AGI evaluationASI riskAI security

Auditable evidence in RAG & agentic systems

Work such as AutoNutriFact studies how claims can be checked against retrieved evidence, how tool actions can be constrained, and how validation decisions can be audited. These controls support verification; they do not prove that an agent cannot hallucinate.

Agent evaluationEvidence

Multimodal & visual AI

Connecting images, language, video, and 3D representations through computer vision, motion analysis, and auditable evidence.

Computer vision3D + language

Transparent cognitive pedagogy

The LLM Story and my computing education tools make model internals inspectable. Students connect visual marks to calculations, question plausible explanations, and practice checking claims against reproducible evidence.

VisualizationStudent research

Compression & scalable AI

Parametric matching, grammar-aware data processing, and reducing CPU-bound parsing overhead in large-scale deep learning pipelines. This work connects compression research with distributed computing.

05 / About

Where research meets
the classroom.

I’m Julie. My work brings together computing, education, and a long-standing interest in how complex systems behave.

I am a Senior Lecturer in Computer Science and Technology at Kean University, where I teach undergraduate and graduate courses and mentor student researchers. I am also pursuing a Ph.D. in Electrical and Computer Engineering, specializing in machine learning, at Rutgers University.

My background spans a Ph.D. in Finance, software development in laboratory and university settings, and more than a decade of teaching. Today, I focus on the security and trustworthiness of AI systems, multimodal learning, and making emerging technologies accessible to students.
