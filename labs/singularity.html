From recursive optimization
to an auditable Singularity.

The engineering question: can verification keep pace with autonomy?

I study containment as a verification challenge: defining what an agent may change, recording what it does, and interrupting actions that exceed its authorization. If recursive improvement accelerates decisions beyond human review, checks must operate before consequential actions, with explicit assumptions and testable boundaries.

Research direction: verifiable bounds on specified systems and policies. Neither this demonstration nor the linked work establishes provable containment or alignment of arbitrary superintelligence. A technological singularity remains a hypothetical transition, not a measured date or capability threshold.

Inspect the equations, policy and limits

Agents N = 2k. Each agent proposes one action per 8 seconds, so the aggregate inter-arrival interval is 8000/N milliseconds. One reviewer takes H milliseconds per action; load ρ = HN/8000. A load above 1 means arrivals exceed review capacity. At 1 there is no spare capacity.

The illustrative pre-action gate holds all proposed actions if k > 4 or ρ > 1. Otherwise actions pass this model’s two checks. Offered token rate assumes 32 tokens per action; it is not observed model throughput. Without the gate, an overloaded review queue grows by N/8 − 1000/H actions per second.

The high-depth compiler loops symbolize iterative generation; the demo does not synthesize code, check ASTs, detect collusion, or prove alignment. A real containment claim needs a precise specification, a trustworthy enforcement boundary, and independent validation.

ASI risk scenario matrix

Expand a scenario to inspect its assumptions and research connections. These are operational design sketches informed by the research agenda, not validated detectors or formal proofs from the Springer chapter.

Scenario / vectorFailure modeCandidate tripwire / mitigation
01 / Recursive driftSelf-modification expands the authorization envelope.Review code changes; enforce depth and tool limits.
Attacker & asset
An agent or injected instruction requests new tools or rewrites generated code. Protected assets are host resources and delegated capabilities.
Trust boundary & invariant
A trusted controller mediates every tool invocation. Requested capabilities must be a subset of the caller’s grant; recursion depth must remain within a fixed budget.
Candidate controls
AST and dependency review, isolated compilation, constrained runtime permissions, and an immutable authorization monitor. Passing compilation alone does not establish safety.
Residual risk
Sandbox escapes, unmodeled effects, compromised monitors and time-of-check/time-of-use gaps can invalidate the assumptions.

02 / Information asymmetryHidden meaning or collusive messages evade a single representation.Inspect provenance and cross-modal inconsistencies.
Attacker & asset
Cooperating agents embed instructions in multilingual or multimodal messages. The protected asset is the integrity of inter-agent decisions.
Trust boundary & failure condition
External messages are evidence, not authority. Failure occurs when an untrusted message grants capability or drives an action without validation.
Candidate controls
Constrain communication channels, retain source traces, compare independent evidence, and require authorization before action. Cross-modal agreement is one consistency check, not proof that a message is benign.
Residual risk
Covert channels and mutually consistent false evidence can pass consistency checks. The linked Matryoshka work is about physical evidence; it is not a validated detector of agent collusion.

03 / Epistemic poisoningCoordinated sources corrupt memory or retrieved evidence.Trace citations; quarantine untrusted writes.
Attacker & asset
An adversary inserts or edits retrieved documents or shared memory. The protected asset is the chain from source evidence to accepted claims.
Trust boundary & invariant
Retrieved text cannot authorize tools. Every accepted claim needs a reviewable source identifier and an explicit support judgment; memory writes require separate authorization.
Candidate controls
Track document provenance and versions, distinguish independent sources from copies, audit claim-to-source links, and abstain when support is insufficient.
Residual risk
A valid citation can still point to false content. Evidential scoring does not guarantee truth or eliminate hallucination.
